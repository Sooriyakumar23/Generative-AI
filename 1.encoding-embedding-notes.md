💡 How computers understand text



Computers speak numbers — not words. Everything starts by converting raw text into mathematical representations a machine can process.



Raw text → tokens → numbers → vectors → meaning



━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

🔧 What happens BTS before the LLM even reads your text?



1. Lowercasing & punctuation removal

2. Tokenization (word/subword/character)

3. Stop word removal

4. Stemming / Lemmatization

5. Vocabulary building

6. Encoding or Embedding the tokens



Only then does the model see your input.



━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━



⚖️ Encoding vs Embedding — why both matter



Encoding → rule-based, sparse, interpretable

Embedding → learned, dense, semantic



Think of Encoding as a dictionary lookup.

Think of Embedding as understanding context.



━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━



📊 Encoding techniques



✅ One-Hot Encoding

Each word → a binary vector. Simple, but no semantic meaning. "King" and "Queen" are equally distant from each other.



✅ Bag of Words (BoW)

Count word frequencies in a document. Loses word order but captures presence.



✅ TF-IDF

Weights rare-but-important words higher. Great for information retrieval (classic search engines).



━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━



🧠 Embedding techniques



✅ Word2Vec (2013)

Learns word associations: "King - Man + Woman ≈ Queen"



✅ GloVe

Global context-aware word vectors from co-occurrence matrices.



✅ FastText

Works on subwords — handles typos and rare words well.



✅ BERT / Transformer Embeddings

Context-aware — "bank" means different things in different sentences.
